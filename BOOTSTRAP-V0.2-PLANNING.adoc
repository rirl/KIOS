= Bootstrap v0.2 Planning
:toc:
:sectnums:

This document does not define Bootstrap v0.2.

It records the evidence and reasoning that justify changes introduced into
Bootstrap v0.2.

Each proposed bootstrap change should be traceable to observations made during
Bootstrap Validation Cycle 1.

Bootstrap revisions are evidence-driven.

Bootstrap evolution follows architectural discoveries rather than architectural
preferences.

The governing question is:

____
What did Bootstrap Validation Cycle 1 teach us?
____

== Candidate Bootstrap Improvements

=== Vision as a First-Class Artifact

Observation

The original architectural vision and the governing specification evolved into
distinct artifacts during Bootstrap Validation Cycle 1.

Evidence

* The original "Architecture as Governance" vision prompt served as the
  architectural vision rather than the execution specification.
* The governing specification became significantly simpler once separated from
  the broader architectural vision.

Conclusion

Vision should become a first-class architectural artifact.

Bootstrap Change

Introduce `VISION.adoc` as part of the canonical bootstrap.

=== Shared Understanding as a First-Class Architectural Concern

Observation

During Bootstrap Validation Cycle 1, one participant recognized that they were
successfully executing the collaborative process without yet understanding how
the individual activities related to the architectural vision or to the larger
community praxis.

This revealed that successful execution of a collaborative process does not
necessarily imply shared understanding of that process.

Evidence

During the validation cycle a participant observed:

____
"I think the AI is doing a good job of following the process, but I don't
understand why we're doing what we're doing. I feel like I'm just following
instructions without understanding the bigger picture."
____

Rather than continuing with the planned sequence of activities, the community
paused the validation cycle to examine the observation itself.

This reflection revealed that the methodology had successfully governed the
creation of architectural artifacts while providing insufficient support for
participants to internalize the architectural vision, the reasoning behind
individual activities, and the relationship between those activities and the
larger architectural purpose.

Conclusion

The first bootstrap validation cycle demonstrated that disciplined
architectural artifacts alone are insufficient.

A community of practice must also cultivate shared understanding.

Architectural progress should not outpace participant understanding.

The collaborative praxis therefore requires explicit mechanisms that help
participants:

* internalize the architectural vision,
* understand the reasoning behind collaborative decisions,
* recognize how individual activities contribute to the larger architectural
  purpose,
* periodically confirm that the community shares a common understanding before
  architectural evolution continues, and
* cultivate the ability to evaluate and improve the architecture together.

The methodology must therefore govern not only the production of architectural
artifacts but also the cultivation of architectural understanding.

Bootstrap Change

Introduce artifacts and practices that cultivate shared architectural
understanding as a first-class concern of the community praxis.

Possible additions to the canonical bootstrap include:

* `VISION.adoc`
+
A stable articulation of the architectural vision that provides enduring
context for all subsequent artifacts.

* Lessons Learned
+
A governed mechanism for participants to articulate what they now understand
that they did not understand before the current validation cycle.

* Community Praxis Diagram
+
A visual representation of the collaborative methodology showing how
conversation, reflection, observations, evidence, lessons learned,
architectural discoveries, warrant, and architectural evolution relate to one
another.

* Collaborative Process Guide
+
A practical guide describing how participants engage in disciplined
architectural learning and how the various architectural artifacts interact.

* Methodological Disciplines
+
A concise statement of the disciplines that define participation within the
community of practice and govern collaborative architectural learning.

* Reflection Prompts
+
Structured questions asked at appropriate checkpoints during each validation
cycle to ensure that architectural progress does not outpace participant
understanding.

These artifacts and practices exist to cultivate shared understanding rather
than merely document architectural decisions.

Rationale

Knowledge may be preserved within a repository.

Architectural warrant may be preserved through provenance, evidence, and Git
history.

Understanding, however, exists only within participants.

A durable knowledge system therefore requires not only governed artifacts but
also a governed community praxis that continually cultivates, evaluates, and
renews shared understanding.

Without shared understanding, coordinated activity risks becoming mechanical
execution rather than disciplined architectural learning.

The purpose of the community praxis is therefore not merely to produce better
artifacts, but to cultivate participants capable of understanding, evaluating,
and improving the architecture together.
